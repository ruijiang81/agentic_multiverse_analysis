# Gate definitions

Gates are decided by the orchestrator from artifacts, never by the agent whose
work is being gated. Each decision is appended to `runs/<run_id>/gates.log`
with the timestamp, the artifact hashes, and the reason.

## G0 — reproduction

**Pass condition.** Every headline coefficient in `config.yaml:headline_estimates`
is reproduced within **15 %** of its target, *and* the design quantities
(N, bandwidths, cluster counts, cohort dates) match exactly.

The 15 % band exists only to absorb language-translation error (Stata → Python
or R). In practice the deviation should sit at the level of published rounding;
a deviation that is large but under 15 % is a finding, and it is recorded in
`reproduction.md` with an explanation, not waved through.

**Target hierarchy**, in order of preference:
1. the published table value, with its exact table/column/row location;
2. the authors' own result log shipped in the package;
3. the *package-output analog* — what the authors' code produces on the shipped
   data — when the package ships neither of the above.

Checked by `scripts/gate_g0.py`.
Nothing downstream starts until G0 passes.

## G2 — fork coverage

**Pass condition.** All three hold:
1. every option in `forks.json` carries a verdict in `audit.json`, with a
   stated reason;
2. the recommended grid is within bounds (100–250 cells per identification),
   and every dimension not crossed has a `held_fixed` entry with a reason;
3. the admissible inference method for headline p-values is designated in
   writing, before any estimate exists.

Checked programmatically by `scripts/gate_g2.py`.

## G4 — implementation

**Pass condition.** Zero unresolved BLOCKER findings after the fix-and-re-audit
loop, and every fix is itself re-audited. A MAJOR finding may be closed by a
written rebuttal with evidence; a BLOCKER may not.

Decided by the independent implementation auditor's re-audit, recorded in
`implementation_audit.md` (round 2+) against `fix_log.md`.

## G6 — report integrity

**Pass condition.** Every numeric claim in `report.md` is generated by a
committed script (`make_report.py`) reading the verified CSVs. Numbers typed
from memory fail the gate.

Checked by `scripts/gate_g6.py`, which re-runs the generator and diffs its
output against the committed report.

### G0 tolerance details

Two refinements, both added because a flat relative tolerance misfires on real
published tables.

**Printed precision.** A target also passes if the reproduction lands within
half a unit of the last decimal the config prints for it. Fifteen per cent of
a coefficient printed as `0.000` or `0.0001` is finer than the rounding the
journal already applied, so the relative test alone would fail on noise. The
precision is read from the raw `config.yaml` text, since `0.000` and `0.0`
parse to the same float.

**Non-gating targets.** An estimate may carry `gating: false`. It is still
compared and printed, but it does not decide the gate. Reserve this for
quantities that are not coefficients — adjusted R-squared, pseudo-likelihoods,
counts the paper reports inconsistently with its own data. A coefficient never
gets `gating: false`; if a coefficient will not reproduce, that is a G0 failure
and belongs in the record as one.

### Survival and co-required tests

`survival.py` decides significance from a `sig_joint_5pct` column when the
grid carries one, and from `p_value < 0.05` otherwise. This matters whenever a
fork auditor designates two tests and requires both — reading `p_value` alone
applies the weaker of the two and overstates survival. The rule actually used
is printed and recorded in `grid_survival_headline.json`.

## Artifact freezing

Every gate PASS records a SHA-256 of the run's artifacts in `.frozen.json`,
and `scripts/freeze.py <run> --check` reports any that changed afterwards.

This exists because an upstream agent finished late and rewrote its artifact
after the gate had passed and its consumers had already run: a brainstormer
appended a 172nd option to `forks.json` hours after the fork auditor had
judged 171 of them and the grid had executed on that audit. G2 caught it only
because its coverage check happens to be cheap to re-run — an edit that kept
the option count would have passed unnoticed and silently invalidated
everything downstream.

Run the check before reading a report. A changed artifact means re-running the
gate that covered it, not reasoning about whether the change "probably"
mattered.
